Proposal · measuring now

AI glasses are the one AI channel security cannot see.

Every AI control in a building assumes the AI runs on something the organization can observe: a network, a browser, an endpoint, an API key. AI glasses use none of those. GlassesGate Zones is a proposed low-cost room sensor that answers one question for a security team: is there an AI-glasses wearer in this space who we did not approve?

The problem

A camera and microphones on a face, backhauled over a personal phone

AI glasses look like ordinary eyewear. What they capture goes over Bluetooth to the wearer's own phone and from there over cellular to a consumer cloud. EssilorLuxottica reported more than 7 million Meta AI glasses sold in 2025. Policy has long assumed that recording means lifting a phone; that assumption no longer holds, for staff, visitors, vendors or contractors.

The published advice has converged: don't frame it as ban-versus-allow; write a tiered policy with open, controlled and restricted zones, cover visitors, and keep an accommodation path for prescription and assistive devices. What is missing is a way to enforce a tier without wireless monitoring built and priced for classified facilities.

The proposal

Two signals, never confused

A small sensor (a laptop now, a Raspberry Pi-class box later) sits in the room and listens to Bluetooth. It never connects to, probes or interferes with any device. It combines a guess with a proof and keeps them apart on every screen and in every log line.

Detection · a guess

Something that looks like AI glasses is in range

Passive listening for Bluetooth advertisements, matched against a signature file where every entry cites the measurement it came from. False positives and misses are expected: VR headsets share manufacturer IDs, and glasses that are switched off broadcast nothing.

The strongest thing it may ever say: "Probable AI glasses nearby." Never who, never "recording", never "confirmed".

Approval · a proof

A wearer we enrolled is here, wearing the enrolled pair

Built on GlassesGate, which already turns a pair of Meta glasses into a presence-bound credential using Meta's official Wearables Device Access Toolkit. The wearer's phone broadcasts a rotating HMAC-signed beacon, only while the enrolled pair is connected and not reported as taken off. It stops within seconds when they come off.

Enrollment is the accommodation path: an admin approves a pair, by name, for this room.

What the room shows

One colour, one name, one plain sentence

For facilities and front-desk staff, not radio engineers. Each state says what it does not establish, in the same place it says what it does.

CLEAR

The sensor is healthy and heard nothing matching glasses in the last window.

Not "no glasses here". Switched-off glasses are invisible.

APPROVED WEARER PRESENT

A phone holding a credential this sensor issued is in range, with its enrolled glasses live.

Inherits every limit in GlassesGate's SECURITY.md: no hardware attestation, relays work.

UNRECOGNIZED DEVICE PRESENT

More glasses-like devices are nearby than approved wearers.

This does not identify anyone or show that anything is being recorded.

SENSOR DEGRADED

The sensor cannot vouch for the room. Treat it as unknown.

A blind sensor is never shown as clear. A dead adapter that shows green is the worst failure this product can have.

Limits, stated first

What it cannot do

These go first because they are true, and because a sensor that overclaims is worse than none.

  • Powered-off glasses are invisible.This enforces policy among mostly honest people. It is not counter-espionage, and a determined adversary with a non-Bluetooth camera is not seen at all.
  • Detection is a heuristic.Manufacturer IDs cover whole product lines, including VR headsets. Confidence tiers manage this; they do not remove it.
  • It never identifies anyone.And it cannot tell whether anything is being recorded. An alert is a prompt for a polite, policy-based conversation at the door, not an accusation.
  • Signal strength is not distance.Walls leak. A sensor near a corridor hears the corridor.
  • Approval and detection are reconciled by count.If an approved pair goes unheard while an unapproved one is heard, the counts balance. The dashboard says so next to the state.
  • It is not Bastille.Enterprise wireless airspace monitoring is far more capable. The difference here is the approved-wearer allowlist and commodity hardware.
Privacy

Rules the sensor keeps

  • Passive only.No connecting, pairing, probing, scan requests, jamming or spoofing. Receive and classify.
  • No raw Bluetooth addresses at rest.Devices are keyed by a hash with a salt that rotates daily and is never written down, so an unrecognized device cannot be tracked across days or rooms.
  • Classify, then discard.Adverts that match nothing are counted for the health check and dropped. Device names are never stored or shown.
  • One sense.No cameras, no microphones. Approved wearers appear only by the label an admin typed.
  • Tamper-evident log.Events are hash-chained and checkable with one command. Exports to a SIEM are off until configured. No telemetry, ever.
Status

Measure before building

The approved-wearer half exists today as GlassesGate. The zone sensor is a proposal with its design written down, and its first milestone is the honest one: find out what the glasses actually broadcast, and in which states, before writing any detection claim.

  1. M0Measure. Passive capture, analysis and scrubbing tools are built and tested. Captures of two pairs in eight states, a distance sweep and negatives are next.Tools done · captures next

If glasses that are worn, connected and idle turn out not to be detectable, this page will say so, and the product narrows to what is.
